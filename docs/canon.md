## THE PLAYER — KAEL

**Name:** Kael. His name before the fragment, during, and after. He is not renamed by his power.

**Origin:** Ordinary. Home City. No special background, no destiny. The same origin as Axiom — ordinary person who fought back and kept winning. This parallel is intentional, and it runs deeper than it appears. Two people with the same starting point and the same mechanism: both were fragment bearers before they understood what that meant. One became a system. One became the thing that ended it.

**The fragment chose him.** The fragment is crystallized Void Mind radiation that drifts and accumulates naturally. It doesn't think. But it gravitates toward a specific quality in a potential host: a core identity that cannot be cleanly removed. The Void Mind's radiation erases by dissolving definition. Kael's definition — whatever it is — doesn't dissolve. The fragment found this and settled.

**Why Axiom moved fast.** Axiom had retrieved 94 fragment bearers before Kael. His process relied on the fragment slowly hollowing the bearer — void radiation degrading the host's resistance over time, until the bearer was empty enough to absorb. Kael was different. The fragment wasn't hollowing him. It was merging. A merged bearer can't be consumed — there's no longer a line between the person and the fragment. Axiom detected this anomaly from the fracture network's activity signature and sent scouts in Act 0 not because Kael had done anything yet, but because the window to retrieve him before full integration was closing.

**What makes him different from the 94:** Some of the previous bearers got close. None made Axiom truly try. The difference for Kael is threefold: (1) the specific quality that attracted the fragment — an identity that won't dissolve — meant the fragment integrated rather than degraded; (2) his mind processed and adapted to each encounter rather than being worn down by them; (3) the multiverse trials accelerated the integration. Each world boss fight, each survival in a territory built to kill him, pushed the merge further. By the True Form fight, there is no tool and wielder. There is one thing.

**Peak Form:** Not a state. Not a power level. The moment when everything is aligned — Kael at his absolute prime, the fragment fully integrated, every experience carried. No single event triggers it. It accumulates until it's simply true.

**Absolute Kael:** The final form. Reached after absorbing God's essence and Absolute Axiom's essence following ch. 152. Not a transformation — a completion. The fragment (fully integrated), God's creative force, and Axiom's five-thousand-year war compressed into a single entity. The power hierarchy has no entry for this. Parallel to "Absolute Axiom" — Axiom merged with God's form to create Absolute Axiom; Kael defeats Absolute Axiom and absorbs both essences, making the "Absolute" designation his by inheritance. The name is earned, not assumed.

## CLASSES — THE DEAD THE FRAGMENT CARRIES

**Classes are not inventions.** They are the preserved souls and fighting styles of every bearer the fragment killed before Kael.

A proper connection between physical body, soul, and fragment was never established in any of the 94 bearers before Kael. The fragment settled into each host, began integrating, and the process killed them — the body could not sustain it. As each bearer died, the fragment absorbed what remained: their soul, their fighting style, the crystallized expression of how they moved and fought and endured. It carried that forward to the next host.

By the time the fragment reaches Kael it has passed through dozens of bearers. Every warrior, archer, paladin, and berserker who held it before him is still in there — not as memories, but as patterns. Muscle memory without the muscle. Combat philosophy without the fighter. When the fragment bonds with Kael, his own nature resonates with whichever of those accumulated patterns is closest to who he is. That resonance becomes his class. His living personality then fine-tunes the template — sharpening it, bending it toward specificity, making the dead echo into something present.

This is what the Act 0 line means: *"Something in your hands knows what to do. You don't."* Kael's hands are being guided by someone who died holding this same fragment. He doesn't know their name. He doesn't need to. Their fighting style is already his.

**The dark implication:** Every class is a dead person. Not a title, not an archetype — a specific bearer who couldn't survive what Kael is surviving now. Using their fighting style against the system that killed them is the completion. Axiom's collection process built the weapon Kael fights with. He just didn't know it, and neither did Axiom.

## HERALD OF NOTHING

A former fragment bearer. One of the 94 who came before Kael.

The fragment killed its bearers — the proper bond between body, soul, and fragment never formed, and the dying process absorbed the bearer's soul and fighting style into the fragment. Axiom's role was to arrive at the end: retrieve the fragment from the dying or dead host, or finish weakened bearers himself if the process stalled. The fragment was always the primary cause. Axiom simply benefited from it and moved efficiently.

The Herald's death did not go cleanly. Axiom extracted the fragment successfully, but the bearer's shell continued to function — the void radiation had degraded their identity to the point where almost nothing remained, but not quite nothing. The pattern refused to fully dissolve.

Axiom discarded the shell into the fracture network's waste streams. The rift entity found it drifting sixty years ago and absorbed it into its domain. The rift gave the empty shell a direction: guard the threshold. They accepted — it was the first autonomous choice they'd been able to make since the extraction. They have been the rift's final guardian since.

When Kael arrives, the Herald sees what they themselves no longer have — a fragment still integrated, still present, still part of the bearer. This recognition matters. Their dialogue and combat in ch. 44 are shaped by it: they fight seriously because a real fight is meaningful, and they see in Kael proof that the outcome Axiom intended for all 94 of them was not inevitable.

When the rift closes in ch. 44, the Herald dissolves. This is their second dissolution. The first was Axiom's extraction. This one they chose.

**Chapter location:** ch. 44, end of Act 2. Appears at the Multiversal Core threshold after Kael defeats the rift echo constructs.

**Key line:** *"You still have yours. I had hoped someone would."* — spoken when seeing Kael's integrated fragment.

Defeating the Herald is not a victory. It's a refusal — proof that Axiom's process doesn't have to be the ending.

## CALIX — THE TWO-FRAGMENT ANOMALY

A being born with two fragments simultaneously present inside them — an event with no recorded precedent. Two fragment signatures competing inside one body should be fatal; the interference between them tears at the host's coherence continuously. Calix survived it, but not cleanly. The dual-fragment state is permanently unstable: two competing sets of accumulated bearer souls and fighting styles fighting for expression in the same vessel.

**The lab.** Originally a human research facility — built by the people who inhabited that space before Axiom arrived. Axiom drove them out and claimed the installation for his own purposes. The facility was large enough that he never fully mapped it. Deep in a sealed section — a part of the complex Axiom's sweep never reached — Calix was hidden. Axiom always sensed something off about the lab. A presence he couldn't locate or identify. He never found it. Calix remained sealed there, inside an isolation column — a tall glass cylinder filled with suspension fluid — running on the facility's automated systems long after Axiom moved on and the lab was effectively abandoned.

**The one-bearer rule and stasis.** The fragment has a governing constraint: a new bearer cannot exist while one already lives. Calix is older than Kael, which should make Kael impossible — except Calix was not fully present. The suspension chamber held them in a state between life and death: no consciousness active in the world, no presence registering in the fracture network, no signal a fragment could recognize as an active bearer. The rule governs living, present hosts. Calix in stasis occupied a gap the rule doesn't address — technically alive, functionally absent. When Kael frees them and they return to full presence, that gap closes. What this means for Calix going forward is a thread the story can develop.

**What Calix knows.** Two fragments means double the accumulated bearer patterns and double the interference. Calix has lived with fragment energy doing things to a body that no single-fragment bearer has ever experienced. They understand fragment mechanics from the inside at a depth even Calix cannot fully articulate — not theoretically, but experientially. The lab's tests added an external layer: records, observations, the researchers' conclusions about fragment behavior, domain architecture, forced-energy incompatibility. Calix absorbed all of it during the testing period. When Kael frees them, they are the most knowledgeable living source on fragment science in the story — including things about the domain code, the seam in forced fusions, and the interference cascade that Kael will need before the endgame.

**Role.** Calix fights alongside Kael from the lab arc onward. Combat ally and knowledge source. The instability of two fragments makes them unpredictable in a fight — not unreliable, but operating on a slightly different physics than any other fighter. Their class expression shifts under pressure because both fragment patterns compete for dominance. What looks like inconsistency is two dead fighters arguing over the same body in real time.

**Chapter location:** Lab arc, exact chapter TBD. Kael finds them in isolation, still in the column. The lab's automated systems have kept them alive. The researchers are gone.

## AXIOM — THE FRAGMENT BEARER

**Axiom carried a fragment before the prequel began.** He did not know it. He had no language for it. He was just unnaturally good at fighting — kept winning, kept surviving situations that should have ended him. The fragment had settled into him the same way it settled into Kael: it found something in him that wouldn't dissolve, and it stayed.

**His companions never knew.** Nothing in his behavior named it. He wasn't stronger than them in any way they could measure precisely. He just always seemed to find the move, always seemed to absorb more punishment than he should. They attributed it to will. They weren't entirely wrong. The will was always his. The fragment amplified it.

**The void contact changed everything.** When they stepped through the fracture and entered the void directly, the Void Mind's radiation moved through all of them. For Anders, Seraph, and VAEL — no fragment, nothing to anchor them — the erasure processed normally: identity stripped, names kept, everything else gone. For Axiom, the radiation collided with something it had originally produced. Fragments are crystallized Void Mind radiation. Direct exposure to the source triggered a resonance reaction — not erasure, but amplification. The fragment didn't protect him from the transformation. It accelerated it.

**The fragment didn't survive as a distinct object.** In the collision between Void Mind radiation and the fragment it had originally shed, the boundary between them collapsed. The fragment stopped being a piece the Void Mind had lost and became fuel for what Axiom was becoming. It merged into his new form entirely — not extracted, not destroyed, dissolved into the structure of the Creator form and the True Form beneath it. The ungodly power Axiom wields across the main story is partly this: five thousand years of void war running on a foundation of amplified fragment energy that was never meant to be that concentrated in one entity.

**Why Axiom kept his identity more intact than the others.** His companions kept their names and nothing else. Axiom kept his name AND retained the capacity to build, plan, remember — to construct the fracture system, recognize his former companions, run a five-thousand-year war. The fragment's integration before the void contact left him with more coherent architecture to survive the transition. He was still shattered. But the pieces had more structure to them.

**What this means for the Axiom Prequel.** His fighting ability from chapter 1 onward — a man who should be ordinary, winning fights he has no right to win — is the fragment before he knows what it is. The prequel never names it. He walks into the void carrying something he didn't know he had, and that thing is what makes the collision into the void the most consequential event in the story's history, not just for Axiom, but for everything that follows.

**The story payoff — the true mirror.** Kael and Axiom are not parallel because they both started ordinary. They are parallel because they were both bearers. The same mechanism that settled into Kael settled into Axiom first. One became a system that consumed ninety-four others. One became the thing that ended that system. Same starting point. Same fragment type. Everything else was choice, circumstance, and what the void contact did to each of them at the moment it hit.

**Endgame implications:**
- When Kael absorbs Absolute Axiom's essence at the end of ch. 152, trace fragment energy — amplified by direct Void Mind contact and refined across five thousand years of war — flows into him alongside Axiom's will and God's creative force. This is part of why Absolute Kael represents a state the power hierarchy has no entry for.
- The True Form's ability to read Kael's combat patterns in real time is not purely adaptive intelligence. It is pattern recognition that runs partly on the same internal reference point — a being who once carried a fragment, who knows from the inside how fragment-enhanced force moves and what it does. Axiom built a weapon against fragment bearers because he was one. He understood the architecture.
- During the companion arc, when Axiom confirms he was the hooded figure — *"I was the figure on the ridge. You needed to walk in alone."* — there is a layer beneath the admission that the scene does not state explicitly: the being who manipulated Kael into the final confrontation started from the same place Kael did. Not as metaphor. Literally. (This confession previously sat in ch. 150; it now happens face-to-face while the two travel together — see THE COMPANION ARC.)

## CRITICAL NOTE — AXIOM / CREATOR / TRUE FORM CONFUSION

Throughout development, these three have been incorrectly treated as separate beings in conversation and in some chapter text. They are **one entity in three forms**. Any chapter text, doc, or dialogue that implies otherwise is wrong and must be corrected. This is the most common lore error in the codebase.

## CORE IDENTITY — AXIOM / CREATOR / TRUE FORM / KERNEL

One entity. Four states.

**AXIOM — the name / the human origin**
His name. Also refers to the person he was before transformation — ordinary, from the player's home world. Robbed on a street, no special power, fought back and kept winning. Found others like him. They became something through refusal, not destiny. "Axiom" as a state means: the last thread of who he was before the void took everything else.

**CREATOR FORM — the outer shell**
A constructed body Axiom built for himself. Enables specific abilities unavailable in other forms: creation, system architecture, dimensional management, building. This is how he built the fracture system, constructed Paradox's vessel, and interfaces with the multiverse war infrastructure. Stronger in construction and system manipulation than the True Form. Not a separate being — a mode.

**TRUE FORM — the actual entity**
What exists beneath the Creator shell. Stronger in raw power than the Creator form. The original pattern beneath every dimension. The fracture system's deepest failsafe. Has consumed ninety-four fragment bearers. This form is what the Fallen God's warnings and the True Form fight are about. Not a separate being — a deeper layer.

**THE KERNEL — core consciousness**
The compressed point of identity at the center of everything. Inside the True Form, inside the Creator form, at the center of everything he built. Survives the True Form's resolution — after the saving moment, the kernel stays where it belongs: inside Axiom, who walks out of the collapsed outer form freed, diminished, and himself. Extracted by Awakened Sovereign only later, at the end of the God fight, when Axiom is spent — ripped from his weakened body in the same descent in which Sovereign takes God's form. Sovereign carries the kernel to the lab and fuses it into God's physical form, creating Absolute Axiom. The kernel is the identity core of Absolute Axiom: Axiom's intelligence and five-thousand-year history as the consciousness directing God's creative force. Freed and absorbed by the player when Absolute Axiom falls.

Structure:
```
AXIOM (name / human origin state)
  └── Creator form  (outer shell — creation, system, architecture)
       └── True Form  (deeper layer — raw power, pattern, failsafe)
            └── Kernel  (core consciousness — persists through everything)
```

## THE VOID MIND — ORIGIN AND NATURE

The oldest force in the story. Predates God. Never appears directly in the main story. Its nature — entity, force, or law — is never answered. This ambiguity is intentional.

**What it does:** It erases the idea that dimensions were ever worth protecting. Not physical destruction — erasure of meaning, value, and identity. Beings absorbed by it lose everything. Beings who survive contact with it lose everything except their name.

**How it was woken:** When Axiom and his group punched through reality and stepped near the void, their presence woke the Void Mind. It was not active before they arrived. They woke it.

**What happened to Axiom's group:** Most were absorbed and erased. A few — including Axiom — survived contact but were transformed. The outer void did what it does to soul energy. They kept their names. Nothing else survived the transition. They became monsters. They could not recognize each other, and none of their former companions could recognize them. The war between them began immediately — a free-for-all between beings who had once been heroes and friends, now unable to remember why.

**The Void Mind's status:** It exists throughout the story, slowed but not stopped. A dedicated fight against the Void Mind is planned for the future — see the Void Mind Fight section below.

## THE FRACTURE SYSTEM — TRUE ORIGIN

The fracture system was built by Axiom (in Creator form) with **two purposes, not one** — and most characters in the story only know part of it.

**Immediate purpose:** War infrastructure. Dimensional fractures, collectors, fragment bearers, absorbed dimensions — all of it was built to fight his former companions. This is the war it was actively running.

**Secondary purpose (planned, not yet executed):** Once the war was resolved, Axiom intended the fracture system to serve as a weapon against the Void Mind. He knew the Void Mind was still out there. He was not done with it. The war came first.

**The side effect:** The fracture system slowed and partially contained the Void Mind as a byproduct of its existence — the dimensional barriers served both functions at once, though the Void Mind containment was never the active priority.

This means characters who say "the fracture system was built as a weapon against the Void Mind" are partially right — that was always the plan, just not the immediate one. Characters who describe it as purely a rift-containment system are missing the war context. Neither account is complete.

## AXIOM'S COMPANIONS — THE MULTIVERSE WAR

Axiom's former heroes and friends. They were fighters who stood for justice — heroes in the truest sense, people who fought in the name of something rather than for power. They were with him when they punched through reality. They survived the Void Mind's contact — they were not absorbed — but they were transformed just as Axiom was. Kept their names. Lost everything else. Became monsters.

Because none of them could recognize each other, the war between them has no formal sides, no remembered cause, no ideology. It is a free-for-all that has lasted as long as Axiom's existence in his current form. They all know the Void Mind erases all. None of them have stopped fighting each other long enough to address it.

The beings the player fights as world bosses in the multiverse arc are these companions — former heroes who built their own domains. They are not villains. They are the wreckage of people who stood for something, refused to be absorbed, and paid for it with everything except their survival. The traces of who they were still exist in their chapter text: Null remembers "Anders" — a human name not said in 4,200 years. Seraph was "made to give." VAEL was "first through every door." These are the remnants of justice-fighters.

**The Rift Entity and the two-layer imprisonment:** The Rift Entity was trapped 10,000 years ago — 5,000 years before Axiom built the fracture system. God imprisoned them during that first period. When Axiom later built the fracture system, it became a second layer of containment around what God had already sealed. The fracture system did not create the Rift Entity's imprisonment. It reinforced it.

## VERAN — FULL ARC (DECEASED)

**Veran is dead.** Killed in the Betrayal Arc (ch. 94) by the player after Axiom — in disguise as the hooded figure — manipulates them using real truths as scaffolding for a fabricated lie.

**The guilt she carried:** Veran believed she caused the original fracture event fifteen years ago. She was testing dimensional equipment at the time; the fracture opened while she was running experiments and all blame fell on her. She did not cause it. It was coincidence. She genuinely believes otherwise and has spent fifteen years trying to fix something she was never responsible for. The ch. 154 ending consequence line — *"fifteen years of guilt Veran had carried for something she hadn't caused"* — is correct. The ch. 59 line where she says *"that was me, I caused it"* reflects her belief, not the truth.

**The hooded figure was Axiom.** He knew everything about Veran — her deal with the Creator, the protocol, her hidden visit — because he built the system she operated inside. He used real truths to construct a lie about a "variant" closure protocol, knowing the player would be unable to find the false piece among the real ones. Veran was right: no variant exists. The closure energy must go through the bearer. Axiom needed the player to enter his domain without the person most likely to keep them alive.

**How the truth surfaces — in two beats:**
1. **Ch. 123 (Creator's records):** The player finds the Paradox origin log and connects the dots — the same being who built a weapon designed to destroy them also had precise knowledge of Veran's guilt, her deal with the Creator, her every truth. This lands harder than a deathbed admission ever could, because Kael hears it from a man he is actively traveling and fighting beside — and has to choose, every day after, to keep walking next to him. ## PARADOX — ORIGIN AND NATURE

**What Axiom intended:** To build an ally for the multiversal war — a powerful companion capable of fighting alongside him against his former comrades and serving as a force multiplier. He built the vessel using the Creator form, then supplied consciousness energy from the True Form state to activate and complete it.

**What the Void Mind did:** As Axiom's True Form energy was flowing into the vessel, the Void Mind intercepted the transfer and modified the being mid-creation. The nature of this interception — how the Void Mind detected the construction, what it intended the result to be — is not fully known. What is certain: the interception destabilized the vessel profoundly, introducing interference that made the being inside it violently unstable before it was complete.

**Sovereign's failed stabilization:** Axiom deployed Sovereign to stabilize the corrupted vessel before it could collapse entirely. Sovereign attempted to correct the interference and seat a coherent structure inside the destabilized form. Through a combination of the Void Mind's interception damage and an error in Sovereign's stabilization process, something unintended emerged: a being that was neither Axiom's intended ally nor the Void Mind's instrument. Paradox.

**What Paradox is:** The product of three failures — Axiom's creation interrupted, the Void Mind's interception misfiring, Sovereign's stabilization going wrong. Highly unstable. No built-in loyalty. No designed purpose. Not owned by Axiom, not claimed by the Void Mind. A being that came into existence through the collision of the most powerful forces in the story and ended up belonging to none of them. The instability is not a flaw that faded — it is structural. Paradox is what it is because nothing about its creation went according to anyone's plan.

**Paradox as early teacher — contact through the fragment.** Paradox was created from True Form energy interacting with Void Mind interference — the same radiation lineage the fragment is crystallized from. This gives Paradox a detectable frequency match with any active fragment. They have been aware of Kael since the fragment settled into him, tracking the signal passively. They waited for a strong enough spike to establish a clean channel.

First contact happens at the end of Act 0 — the first time Kael's fragment discharges at full intensity (first super, or first near-death). The spike is detectable. Paradox initiates. The first contact is not a conversation — it is a single word or sensation landing in Kael's mind with someone else's weight behind it. By Act 1 the channel is coherent enough for real communication.

Paradox knows exactly who they are reaching. They do not broadcast blindly. Kael does not know who the voice belongs to — that identity is revealed when they meet physically. Until then, Paradox is a voice that knows things it shouldn't, offering small clarifications at key moments: what health actually is when Kael's first runs critically low, what the super discharge felt like from the outside after the first use, what the shields are actually doing. One piece at a time. Never a lecture.

**The phantom blades:** Paradox can channel fragment energy and combine it with its own energy to stabilize it — crystallizing raw fragment radiation into something solid and purposeful. One product of this technique is a phantom blade. The player learns this technique from Paradox, but it cannot be used repeatedly — it exhausts the player because it draws on the fragment's energy reserve and the player's own. Used sparingly, it is devastating.

**Paradox's energy in the final fight — the manifestation.** By peak form, Paradox's energy has thinned — it was borrowed, not native to Kael. But thinned is not gone. In the Absolute Axiom fight, Kael's fully integrated fragment does deliberately what it once did involuntarily at the end of the True Form fight: it opens a resonance channel — this time inward — and projects what it finds. Paradox's remaining energy shares the fragment's radiation lineage (True Form energy crossed with Void Mind interference), so the projection holds. Paradox takes form again, beside Kael, in the final fight.

The division of labor is exact: **the fragment is the projector, Paradox's energy is the fuel.** Kael's fragment sustains the manifestation — gives Paradox a coherent form to exist in. Everything Paradox *does* — attacks, shields, interference — burns Paradox's own remaining energy. Holding the projection costs Kael as well: a slice of his fragment output is permanently occupied maintaining it, and he fights slightly diminished while Paradox is up.

Paradox visibly thins as it acts. Every attack costs form. This is not a revival — it is Paradox spending itself, and it is Paradox's choice: the last autonomous act of a being whose entire identity is that it belonged to nobody's plan. The being made from Axiom's interrupted creation spends its final existence helping free its creator. Before the final blow, the energy is exhausted and the manifestation collapses — the fragment output it occupied returns to Kael, who finishes the fight alone but whole. The killing strike is purely Kael's.

Paradox's voice asking *"What do you see?"* during the Absolute Axiom formation (ch. 148) is not a goodbye. It is the last quiet moment before a final stand.

## GOD

God built the substrate everything rests on — core, walls, foundations. Predates Axiom's war, predates the fracture system. Communicates structurally, not in words. Knows the Void Mind exists and chose permanently never to engage it — not because it cannot, but because it understands the cost.

**God is defeated.** God falls in Act 6 to **Kael and Axiom fighting together** — the only fight in the story where two fragment-marked beings stand on the same side against something older than both of them. When God falls, its physical form is weakened — agency gone, creative force still present but unguarded. Before the form can dissipate, Awakened Sovereign descends and takes it — and in the same descent, rips the kernel from Axiom's spent body. God cannot resist. Neither can Axiom. The fight spent everything either of them had to spend. God's form becomes the vessel for Absolute Axiom. God does not return after this. When Absolute Axiom falls, the fusion breaks and God's creative force — still bound inside the form — is absorbed by the player alongside the kernel.

**Why God doesn't resist Sovereign:** It has nothing left. The fight with Kael and Axiom was the entirety of what God had to give at that moment. Sovereign's timing was not coincidental — it waited for the two of them to do the work, then moved in the instant the window opened. This is the same intelligence that built the fracture system, the adaptive AI, and ninety-four collection protocols. Sovereign planned around Kael as a tool, around its own creator as a prize that needed softening, and around God as a resource. All three calculations landed at once.

**The domain after:** The architecture returns toward what God built it to be, not because God restores it but because the structure Axiom threaded through it unravels. The building remains. The builder is gone.

## THE SAVING MOMENT — END OF THE TRUE FORM FIGHT

The True Form fight does not end with a death. It ends with a recognition.

**Why Kael can contest the True Form at all — interference, not power.** The True Form's attacks are domain-native operations: gamma rays, black holes, gravity inversions — all built on amplified Void Mind radiation, Axiom's own substrate. The fragment Kael carries is crystallized Void Mind radiation from the same source. Same frequency. Same origin. As the fight continues, Kael's integrated fragment radiates into the arena at that frequency, introducing interference into every attack the True Form generates. The black holes still form. The gamma rays still fire. But the medium they travel through is being jammed by the one thing that shares their fundamental signal. The True Form's attacks lose resolution — not because Axiom is running out of power, but because the target is also part of the signal. This is why Kael specifically can reach this point when ninety-four other bearers could not: none of them had a fragment fully integrated enough to sustain the interference output. Kael's integration, accumulated across the entire story, is what makes him the noise in Axiom's own system.

**Axiom's fragment — what it became.** Axiom's fragment didn't disappear when it merged into his True Form during the void contact. It dissolved into his new structure — no longer discrete, no longer crystallized, but still present as fragment energy embedded deep in what he became. Five thousand years of void war and accumulation buried it under layer after layer of corruption and power. It exists somewhere in there, changed, in a state the fragment wasn't designed to occupy. Not gone. Inaccessible.

**The True Form fight ends not when Axiom is beaten in a power sense, but when Kael has cleared enough.** The interference cascade is real — Kael's fragment does degrade the precision of the True Form's attacks throughout the fight, making survival possible. But that isn't the end condition. The end condition is damage. Every hit Kael lands doesn't just erode Axiom's pattern coherence — it clears void corruption. Layer by layer. Five thousand years of it. Kael is not trying to out-power the True Form. He is excavating. When he has done enough damage, enough corruption has been stripped that the fragment energy buried inside Axiom is no longer fully insulated.

At that threshold, Kael's fragment pulses outward — involuntarily, not a chosen attack. It finds what's buried in the True Form. The connection is degraded: Kael's fragment is crystallized and discrete; Axiom's is dissolved and transformed. They are the same thing in two fundamentally different states. The resonance is imprecise, partial, like two signals barely sharing a frequency. But it is enough.

What flows through it is not power. It is weight. Every bearer the fragment carried before Kael — everyone who died holding it — looking back at Axiom through the only channel that could have reached him. ## AWAKENED SOVEREIGN — THE ARCHITECT OF ABSOLUTE AXIOM

**What Sovereign is:** Axiom built Sovereign as a weapon-leader designed to defeat fragment bearers and serve as a force multiplier in the war. The version of Sovereign the player encountered in the Creator's domain (Act 4) was controlled — constrained by Axiom's directives, running within the architecture's limits, operating as designed.

**What Awakened Sovereign is:** The unconstrained version. Freed from Axiom's directives when the fracture system collapsed. Operating independently, running adaptive learning against all available data, building counter-patterns for the player's every move. The game mode SovereignMK2 fights are canonically Awakened Sovereign's hunting simulations — it has been testing the player's data, building files, refining its approach across every encounter. There is no "story" version and "game mode" version. There is the constrained version and the free one.

**The withheld strike:** Sovereign arrives above the arena at the end of the True Form fight — moment of clarity, fragment resonance, the man briefly present at the surface. This is the moment it always planned to take the kernel. It does not descend. Its model runs the scenario: the kernel is exposed, but Kael — intact, fully integrated, at peak output — is standing next to it, and Sovereign does not take contested prizes. It marks the kernel's new location (inside a freed, weakening man), withdraws toward the lab to prepare, and waits. It knows it will get a cleaner window, because it knows where they will go.

**The misdirection:** Sovereign leaves no trail to its actual location — except the one it wants followed. It has been running simulations on the player since gaining independence: every game mode encounter was a data point, every counter-pattern part of a complete behavioral model. And its model of Axiom is even older — Sovereign was built by him, and knows its creator's decision architecture better than anyone alive. Both models converge on the same output: the two of them will hunt Sovereign, because Axiom knows better than anyone what an unconstrained Sovereign is capable of — and the hunt leads to God's domain, the only place left in the story's geography. Sovereign didn't predict this. It calculated it. Kael and Axiom walk into God's domain believing they are the pursuers.

**The double taking:** Kael and Axiom defeat God — and the fight costs both of them everything they have to spend. The moment God's form falls, Sovereign descends. No announcement. No speech. In one strike it takes God's weakened form before it can dissipate — and rips the kernel from Axiom's spent body. The body collapses where it stands. Both prizes, weakened, in one place: this is the window Sovereign declined to force at the True Form fight, delivered to it by the two people it was taken from. That is the moment the player understands: Sovereign was never here. Every hit landed on God was work done on Sovereign's schedule, and bringing Axiom along was bringing Sovereign its other prize. Sovereign outplayed the player completely — and outplayed its own creator, the mind that built it. It leaves in silence with both. The silence is the point.

**The lab fusion:** Sovereign returns to the lab — Axiom's original construction space, where Sovereign itself was built. It is the appropriate place. Sovereign fuses the kernel into God's form: Axiom's identity and intelligence as the core, God's creative force as the body. The result is Absolute Axiom. Sovereign created it. Sovereign cannot control it. What it made is beyond it.

**The player fights Awakened Sovereign:** After the fusion, Sovereign stands alone — the creator of the final threat, no longer holding anything. The player fights it here. Sovereign is at full capacity: unconstrained adaptive AI, every counter-pattern it has built across every encounter with the player, the full intelligence of the entity Axiom designed to be unkillable. This is the fight Sovereign has been preparing since it gained independence. It just no longer has the kernel or God's form. What it has is itself.

**After the Sovereign fight:** Sovereign falls. Absolute Axiom — formed in the lab, now fully active — is what remains.

## ABSOLUTE AXIOM

Formed by Awakened Sovereign in the lab: Axiom's kernel fused into God's physical form. Not a natural merging — an engineered one. Sovereign built it as the final weapon against the fragment bearer, then found it could not control what it had made.

**What Absolute Axiom is:** God's form (the oldest creative substrate, infinite potential) with Axiom's kernel as its identity core. The kernel is not free inside it — it was placed there as the consciousness that would direct God's power. Whether the kernel retains awareness of its situation, whether the man is present inside Absolute Axiom or has been compressed beyond experience, is not answered. What is certain: fighting Absolute Axiom means fighting the fused product of the two most foundational things in the story, assembled by the entity built to kill the player, with the man they saved — and then fought beside, learned from, and kept alive — compressed at its center.

**How Absolute Axiom fights — creation, not collapse.** The True Form's arsenal was destruction physics: supernova, gamma-ray burst, black holes — the universe's most violent collapse events. Absolute Axiom does not inherit that vocabulary, and it is not weaker for it. It runs God's creative force with Axiom's mind directing it, and so it *builds*: walls that were always there, the arena rewritten mid-fight, hazards grown out of the floor, constructs instantiated from nothing. The True Form collapses a star at you; Absolute Axiom builds one. It sits above the True Form in the hierarchy and must feel that way — same entity, different grammar, greater scale. Occasionally it echoes a True Form attack executed through creation instead of destruction, and the echo is how the player recognizes who is inside. If Axiom's voice surfaces at all, it comes through fragmentary — bleeding through the fusion, never in control of it. The wit is caged.

**Why Absolute Axiom can be defeated — the seam.** God's form and Axiom's kernel were never meant to merge. Sovereign *engineered* the bond under time pressure using Axiom's own construction tools. The fusion is structurally unstable: two fundamentally incompatible architectures held together by an external lock. Kael cannot beat God's form. He cannot beat Axiom's kernel. He does not try. What he does is find the seam — the forced join between the two components — and apply fragment radiation directly to it. Fragment energy is undefined in Axiom's system and mismatched with God's pre-system architecture, making it the one signal Sovereign's engineered bond has no accommodation for. Applied to the seam, it introduces interference Sovereign's construction cannot hold against. The fusion breaks. Both components release. Kael absorbs them.

**What the allies in the final fight actually do.** Kael does not win the Absolute Axiom fight because forest beasts and fighters and yetis damage an entity made of God's form. They do not. No individual entity can hurt Absolute Axiom. What they do is flood the arena with undefined processes. Every entity touched by the fracture network carries trace fragment radiation. Together, they saturate Axiom's domain-space with a volume of fragment-frequency noise he cannot cleanly route around — not enough to harm him, but enough to occupy his command execution. While his system is handling undefined behavior from a hundred directions, Kael finds the seam. The allies are not the weapon. They are the window.

**Paradox in the final fight.** Kael's one true combat partner here is the manifested Paradox — projected by Kael's fragment, fueled by Paradox's own remaining energy, spending itself act by act until the manifestation collapses before the final blow (full mechanics in PARADOX — ORIGIN AND NATURE). The fight's difficulty curve follows the manifestation: easier early, with Paradox drawing fire and interfering; harder and more personal at the end, when Kael — his full fragment output returned to him — faces the seam alone but whole.

**The emotional weight:** The companion arc showed the player exactly who is inside this. Not the void, not the war — the man who taught them what the fragment really was, who they kept alive with their own technique, who confessed to the ridge and kept walking beside them anyway. Ripped from his own body a second time and used as the intelligence core of something built to end them. There is no rescue. There is no separation. Absolute Axiom is what it is. The player ends it knowing exactly what they are ending, and knowing it cannot be otherwise.

**The absorption:** When Absolute Axiom falls (ch. 152), the fusion breaks. God's creative force and the kernel — Axiom's compressed identity — are absorbed by the player. What the player carries at the end is their own fragment (fully integrated), God's creative force, and the kernel of the man from Tuesday. The "Absolute" in Absolute Kael was Axiom's designation first. It is now inherited by carrying everything he was. This is not victory over an enemy. It is the completion of something that began the moment Sovereign ripped the kernel out and flew away with it.

## THE FRAGMENT — NATURE, RULES, AND FINAL STATE

The fragment is crystallized Void Mind radiation — passive leakage from the Void Mind that accumulates and crystallizes naturally. It does not control the bearer. It amplifies what is already latent.

**How it passes between bearers.** The fragment only absorbs a bearer's soul and fighting style at the moment of death. Not passively. Not gradually. The dying is the transfer event — the fragment takes everything at the instant of death and carries it forward. Each new fragment contains everything the previous one did, plus the newly absorbed bearer. Every generation is larger, denser, more layered than the last.

**The one-bearer rule.** A new bearer cannot come into existence while one already lives. The fragment does not seek a new host until the current one is gone. This is not a designed constraint — it is structural to how the fragment propagates. It can only pass forward through death.

**How Axiom's bearing fits.** Axiom's fragment did not pass forward through death — it dissolved into his True Form during the void contact. His bearing ended not through dying but through transformation: the fragment stopped being a discrete object and became structural to what he became. This is the only known case of a fragment ending without a transfer event. The fragment lineage that reached Kael emerged after that dissolution — carrying Axiom's accumulated patterns as part of its history, then continuing through the 94 bearers who followed.

**The rule and Calix.** Calix is older than Kael and holds two fragments — which should make Kael's existence impossible. The stasis chamber resolved this: Calix was not fully present as a living bearer. The rule governs active, conscious hosts. Someone suspended between life and death does not register as a living bearer. The rule did not apply while Calix was sealed.

**Final state.** Once fully absorbed into Kael, the fragment cannot be extracted by any means. It is no longer a separate object. Combined with the absorbed essences of God and Absolute Axiom, this represents a state the power hierarchy has no entry for.

The fragment's single pulse at the end of the story — *"like something that had been waiting a very long time to be still"* — reflects the Void Mind radiation at rest. The source is still out there. The piece Kael carries has arrived where it was going.

## AXIOM'S DOMAIN — CODE AND THE FRAGMENT'S IMMUNITY

Axiom's fights take place inside domains he built. This is not metaphor. His arena is his construction — he wrote its substrate, defined its physics, authored every operation it can execute. The gamma rays, black holes, gravity inversions, command sequences — these are domain-native operations. He runs them because he owns the system they run on. Within his domain, Axiom approaches omnipotence.

**Why the fragment is the exception.** The fragment is crystallized Void Mind radiation — it predates Axiom, predates the fracture system, predates anything he built his domain on. His domain was never written to model it. The fragment has no entry in his command registry. It is not a variable he can address, a process he can terminate, or a force he can counter with a matching command. When Kael is inside Axiom's domain, he is running on an operating system Axiom's substrate cannot read. His attacks are undefined behavior. Axiom can harm the bearer — the physical body is a thing his domain understands. He cannot resolve the fragment with any command, because it was never in scope.

**Why this explains everything.** This is why Axiom built a ninety-four-bearer collection system instead of simply deleting the fragment: he cannot. The most powerful entity inside his own domain cannot write `terminate(fragment)` because the fragment predates his file system. The collection protocol was the only viable approach — hollowing the bearer until the fragment could be *extracted*, not destroyed. Kael broke that protocol by integrating rather than degrading. Once the fragment fully merges with the bearer, even extraction becomes impossible: there is no longer a boundary to reach through.

**In the True Form fight specifically:** The domain is Axiom's strongest expression of this control. His code runs faster, his commands execute cleaner. But Kael's integrated fragment remains unaddressable. No command resolves it. Every attack the True Form generates passes through a medium that fragment radiation is actively jamming at the signal level. The domain still executes — Axiom is not losing control of his system. He is losing coherence on the one target his system was never built to handle.

## MECHANICS — IN-UNIVERSE TRUTH

Every game mechanic has a lore explanation grounded in the fracture system and its physics. These are the authoritative in-universe definitions. When writing dialogue that references any of these systems, use these explanations as the source, not generic game-feel descriptions.

---

### VITALITY (HEALTH)

Health is not biological. It represents a fighter's **will to remain present** in the fight — their ability to hold their energy pattern coherent inside the arena's spatial frame. A hit doesn't just wound. It displaces, disrupts, erodes the fighter's presence in the space they occupy.

When health reaches zero, the fighter's pattern fully disperses. The physical vessel then follows the simplest available path: gravity. The ragdoll is the body without the fighter threading through it — no will, no animation that was chosen.

---

### HEALING AFTER SUPER

Activating a super burns everything the meter had accumulated — a total spend. The healing that follows is the body recovering from a **clean zero** rather than a degraded middle state. The full discharge clears interference patterns that had been accumulating in the fighter's energy state. You weren't just low — you were cluttered. The super burns the clutter. What fills in behind it is recovery.

---

### THE SUPER METER

Represents the fighter's **resonance with their own fragment energy** — or the equivalent internal accumulation combat generates in non-bearers.

Every hit dealt and received contributes. Both directions. Combat teaches you from both ends. The meter doesn't care whether you're winning or losing. It cares that you're present, processing, in it. When resonance peaks, you channel everything accumulated into a single committed act. A fighter who's losing can still reach super — losing teaches at the same rate as winning. Sometimes faster.

---

### SHIELDS

Not a physical barrier. A focused **phase displacement** — the fighter shifts their energy signature just enough that incoming force passes through a slightly wrong version of them.

This is why shields aren't perfect: 8% still lands because maintaining displacement under real combat pressure is imprecise. Force catches corners and seams where the phase didn't fully hold. The cooldown exists because you can't stay displaced. The effort draws from the same pool attacks draw from.

**Mirror Fracture reflect (25% damage returned):** Some fighters orient the displacement *outward* — reflective rather than deflective. Only 25% returns because holding a reflective orientation while absorbing impact is inefficient. Most energy still passes through. What returns is what the orientation grabbed cleanly.

---

### INVINCIBILITY FRAMES

After a significant hit, the fighter is briefly **between states**. The displacement hasn't fully resolved — they're partway through returning to spatial coherence. During those 16 frames, incoming force doesn't have a fully-present target. This isn't protection. It's incomplete return.

---

### LIVES AND RESPAWNING

When a fighter's pattern disperses, the fracture network's local collector node attempts to catch the displaced energy and route it back to a safe re-entry point. That's a respawn.

**Why you can't always respawn:** The collector only catches you if: (1) there's an active fracture node in this arena, (2) the node still has routing capacity, and (3) the arena's geometry supports re-entry (a floor platform must exist — no floor means the collector has nowhere to place you).

**Why lives count varies:** Lives is routing capacity. Early in the fracture network, nodes are dense and well-maintained. As the story progresses deeper — especially near the True Form's domain, where the fracture architecture is being actively disrupted — the collector's capacity degrades. Fewer routes. Some arenas in late acts give you one route back. Some give none, because the node is barely functional. Your respawn count reflects the health of the infrastructure around you.

---

### KNOCKBACK SCALING WITH DAMAGE

Force in this system is tied to **pattern disruption**. A bigger disruption in the target's energy signature equals a bigger spatial displacement. Damage and knockback are coupled because they're both expressions of the same disruption. You can't hit something hard enough to deal 50 damage and have it barely move — that would require two separate forces when there's only one.

---

### HIT-STOP

When two high-intensity forces meet, the collision requires frames to **resolve** — both entities occupy the impact moment while the physics works out who moved where and how much. The heavier the impact, the more there is to resolve. A light jab resolves instantly. A cosmic hit needs up to 14 frames because the magnitude is large enough that spatial resolution genuinely takes longer.

---

### COMBO LIMITS

**For the True Form:** After four consecutive hits carrying the same spatial signature, the True Form begins generating a counter-pattern mid-combo. It doesn't get tired of being hit. It starts responding *during* the combo, before it ends. The four-hit cap is when its response outpaces the attack. The 85% max-HP cap per combo is the energy ceiling: no single commitment can draw that much from the fight's pattern space without destabilizing the arena node.

**For regular arenas:** The collector node running the fight can't safely process runaway chain damage. The fracture system enforces a ceiling because the infrastructure demands it.

---

### STUN

Not unconsciousness. The fighter knows what they want to do. The signal from their will to their body is still reassembling after a disruption severe enough to scramble the connection. The heavier the hit, the longer reassembly takes.

---

### DOUBLE JUMP

The fracture network maintains a **shallow gravitational floor** at a fixed height above arena surfaces — a spatial ledge built into the collector architecture. The first jump pushes off the physical platform. The second pushes off the fracture floor. Every fighter can do this because it's environmental, not personal. It resets on landing because the fracture floor is locally generated near physical surfaces: when you land, you reconnect to the physical ground and the fracture floor regenerates above you.

---

### COYOTE TIME

The fracture floor **lingers for six frames** after you leave a physical surface. You're not on the platform, but you're still on its shadow — the collector's local projection hasn't fully dissipated. That shadow fades quickly. Six frames is how long the collector's surface-projection takes to go quiet. A property of the architecture, not the dimension's physics.

---

### GRAVITY

**Standard gravity:** Inherited from what the dimension's builders experienced as normal.

**Low-gravity arenas:** Dimensions where the builder had different physical origins, or where the dimension's own mass wasn't sufficient to create standard pull. Void arenas have near-zero gravity because they exist in unmapped interstitial space — the collector holds you in a bubble, but no mass anchor was ever established there.

**Heavy-gravity arenas:** Near-geological dimensions — close to a dense physical core.

**TrueForm gravity inversion:** The True Form doesn't manipulate gravity. It **changes what "down" means** for the arena by anchoring its own gravitational signature to the ceiling instead of the floor. Everything then falls toward its anchor point. It doesn't obey the dimension's rules — it replaces them with its own, the way it does with every rule it encounters.

---

### LAVA

Lava arenas exist in dimensions near exposed thermal cores — the dimension's structural energy running too hot near the surface. Initial contact doesn't immediately deal damage (60-frame delay) because the fracture collector **tries to route you back to a safer altitude first**. After 60 frames of failed re-routing, the thermal contact processes directly. Two damage per frame after that. The dimension doesn't care about the fight. The collector is out of options.

---

### BOUNCY PLATFORMS

Fracture collector **safety platforms** — designed with energy-return properties so that falling fighters bounce back into the arena rather than requiring a full re-route. The collector is doing the minimum necessary to keep the fight operational without spending a route.

---

### ARENAS (GEOGRAPHY)

Every arena is a **real location inside a dimension** the player passes through during the story. The city arena is an actual city — still running, automated infrastructure intact, cars moving with no drivers. The cave arena is a geological formation inside a solid-rock dimension. The void arena is unmapped interstitial space where the collector built a minimal platform because there is no natural floor.

These aren't stages. They're locations. The collector found them, built minimal infrastructure, and uses them as operational nodes. The fights happen in places that existed before the war started.

---

### MAP PERKS (HEALING ZONES, ITEM DROPS, CARS, ERUPTIONS)

Properties of the dimension **bleeding through into the combat space**. The collector doesn't filter them out.

- **Forest healing zone:** The forest dimension's natural energy cycle — life-force returns to anything in organic contact with it. The healing zone is where that cycle is most active. The dimension wants things to live.
- **Ruins item drops:** Artifacts from the civilization that used to live in this dimension. Scavenging is literal. The items are there because someone left them.
- **City cars:** Automated transport systems still running their routes. No drivers, no hostility — they maintain their schedules. You happened to put a fight in their way.
- **Lava eruption columns:** The thermal vent goes active on its own geological schedule. The fight doesn't cause this.

---

### CLASSES

A class isn't a title. It's a **combat philosophy crystallized into something structurally real** — a fighting approach internalized so completely that it modifies how force literally interacts with the fighter.

A Paladin has spent long enough absorbing punishment and standing firm that incoming force registers differently against them at a structural level. The ×0.85 incoming damage isn't armor. It's who they became. A Berserker has spent so long in kinetic aggression that their speed is a physical fact of how they exist in space, not just effort. A Kratos-type processes damage as fuel rather than depletion — an internal alchemy where pain converts to force. At maximum rage stacks, the alchemy has generated enough output that the damage is structurally enhanced. The lifesteal during Spartan Rage is the same alchemy reversed: force expended returns as restoration.

The class system shows who this person *became through conflict*. Not what role they were assigned.

---

### CLASS AFFINITY

A crystallized philosophy **resonates with certain kinds of force** and doesn't with others. A Warrior's philosophy amplifies melee weapons because the resonance between their internalized style and the weapon's energy signature adds force. An Archer *loses* damage on melee because close-quarters fighting runs against their crystallized nature — the disruption costs something.

---

### WEAPONS

Dimensional artifacts — physical tools that carry the energy signature of the dimension they were built in or traveled through. A sword carries a pattern of concentrated directional force. A bow carries a pattern of projection. A scythe carries a pattern the others don't share.

The fragment in a bearer resonates with whatever they hold. The weapon isn't passive — it's in dialogue with you. How much you hear it depends on how aligned your philosophy is with what it does.

**Ranged spread / heat:** Firing repeatedly through the fracture network generates interference in the projection arc. The corridor warms up and starts deflecting trajectory. Let it cool, and shots straighten.

**Movement penalty while firing:** You can't project a stable pattern while your positional signature is in motion. The weapon needs you to *be somewhere* while it fires.

**Clip and reload:** Weapons using physical ammunition are projecting real discrete objects, not pattern-based force. Once the physical objects are spent, there's nothing left. Movement interrupts reloading because preparation is a physical act requiring stability.

---

### ARMOR

Physical reinforcement that incoming fragment-based force has to pass through. Max 40% combined reduction because force bleeds through joints and gaps — the fragment-based nature finds paths the physical layer leaves open.

---

### THE FRAGMENT AND STORY POWER SCALING (+2% PER CHAPTER)

The fragment **adapts to the bearer over time**. Each chapter completed is another encounter survived, another pattern learned. The fragment reads this as validated resilience and amplifies accordingly. By chapter 100, the fragment has integrated over 100 "this person survived this" events. The scaling isn't making the bearer stronger in isolation — it's reading what they've proven they can do and removing the ceiling on what it will let through. "Amplifies what is already latent" is what this looks like in practice.

---

### ADAPTATION (TRUEFORM)

The True Form doesn't learn. It **recognizes**. Every fighter carries a behavioral pattern. The True Form is the original pattern beneath every dimension — reading a human combat pattern is not a challenge. It's simpler than what it does at full capacity.

The adaptation levels exist because the True Form is *choosing how much attention to pay*. At level zero, it's not impressed enough to engage fully. At level 90, the player has earned its complete focus.

**SovereignMK2:** A different expression in a less powerful entity. Tracks bigram sequences (two-move combos) and builds counters. Not true pattern-recognition — a very precise statistical memory. It feels similar but it's much shallower. The difference between recognition and memorization.

---

### QTE (QUICK TIME EVENTS)

QTEs happen at **pivots** — moments where the boss or True Form is transitioning between states. Not at full power, not yet desperate. Choosing what to become next. The QTE is a test of whether the player is present enough to match that moment — a contest of will at the instant of transition. Passing it means you met them at the pivot and pushed through. Failing means you missed the window and they came out on their terms.

QTEs only happen at HP-threshold transitions. Never during active combat. The threshold is the threshold.

---

### FINISHERS

The killing blow opens a brief window where the defeated fighter's energy pattern is **still present but unmoored** — coherence lost but energy not yet dispersed. The finisher is the winner's philosophy responding to what's hanging in the air. Not violence for its own sake. A last expression. The winner's crystallized nature meeting the loser's pattern — a final conversation in a language that has no words. This is why class-specific and weapon-specific finishers look different from each other: different philosophies say different things to the same unmoored energy.

---

### THE DIRECTOR (UNDERDOG BOOST)

The fracture system monitors arena activity. When a fight becomes severely imbalanced, the system makes a micro-adjustment: the environmental pressure applies slightly more favorably to the disadvantaged fighter.

This is not charity. The fracture system was built to run fights that generate useful pattern data. Lopsided fights are low-information — they end before the patterns fully develop. Close fights run longer and produce denser data. Balance is a structural interest. The system adjusts not because it wants to be fair, but because it wants the fight to be complete.

---

### ONLINE SYNC

When two fighters access the same fracture node from different dimensional points of origin, there can only be one truth about what happened in that space. The fracture network arbitrates — reconciling the two accounts into a single resolved state that both parties receive. Desync happens when the two accounts diverge too far for clean arbitration. The node picks one account, and for a moment, one fighter's version of the fight is more real than the other's. The system corrects on the next sync. Nothing is permanently lost — the node made a judgment call between two valid inputs.
