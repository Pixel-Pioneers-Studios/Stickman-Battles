# SOVEREIGN PREQUEL — STRUCTURE

> Status: SPEC, NOT BUILT. Companion to `docs/sovereign-prequel-concept.md` (the pitch).
> This file takes the pitch's five acts and resolves them into concrete chapters in the schema the
> Axiom Prequel already runs on (`axiom-prequel/js/story.js` — `CHAPTERS[]`, alternating
> `type: 'interlude'` / `type: 'play'`). Nothing here overrides `docs/canon.md`.
> Chronology: ~50,000 years before the main story, ~45,000 before the Axiom Prequel.

---

## CORRECTION TO THE PITCH (read this first)

The concept doc places the "third seam" beat — the second brain answering half a second late — at the
**end of Act III**, but it also has the second brain **built in Act V**. Those cannot both be true.

**Resolution used here:** the seam beat moves to the very end of Act V, after construction, as the
last playable moment before the ending interlude. It loses nothing — the beat's power was never its
position, it was that it goes unremarked — and it gains the correct reading: the first crack appears
in the thing he built to end his loneliness, within sight of the war he thinks he just won.

Act III keeps a structurally identical *unremarked* beat so the act still lands: the **ledger's first
silent omission** — an entry he files that isn't there when he looks for it. Same grammar, different
culprit, and it reads on replay as God, not as the brain that doesn't exist yet.

`docs/sovereign-prequel-concept.md` should be corrected to match before anything is built.

## PER-CHAPTER BEATS

### Act I — The Man Who Finished Everyone's Sentences

**0 — Rain Before It Rains** (interlude, ~1800 wide, `background: 'village_day'`)
A walk across a village at dusk. Dialogue is other people's, and his line always arrives a half-beat
before theirs justifies it. No narration explains this. He is not shown to be pleased by it.
Deliberately does **not** reuse the Tuesday device — that word belongs to Kael and Axiom, and a third
use spends it. The shared grammar here is the *ordinariness*, not the vocabulary.

**1 — The Feud** (read+play, ~2200)
Two families and a water channel. Read phase: observe both patriarchs' patrol/temper loops and commit
one structural edit (where a gate is left open). The fight that follows is small, ugly, four men with
farm tools, and the player's edit decides who is already winning when it starts.

**2 — Nobody Asked Him To** (interlude)
The village credits the wrong man for the resolution. He says nothing. Canon's isolation line is the
authorial target — he never once describes it as loneliness, so no line in this chapter may.

**3 — The Market Bends** (read+play, ~2600)
A rival merchant hires a blade. Read phase has a harder target (a trained fighter, not a farmer).
First combat with real threat. Tutorialises that the read, not the swing, is the skill.

**4 — Just Visible Enough** (interlude)
The closing beat from the pitch, verbatim in intent: a single redirected outcome that should have been
unpredictable. End the chapter on an ordinary image, held one beat too long. Nothing acknowledges it.

### Act II — Controlled Chaos

**5 — A Board That Still Burns** (read+play, ~3200)
Town scale, multiple simultaneous reads. The design goal is *appetite*: this chapter should be fun in
a way the player feels slightly implicated by. Canon is explicit it was never destruction — the win
condition must be a board still running, still surprising, entirely his.

**6 — Bad Luck, Consistent** (interlude)
Three throwaway failures, spaced so a player tracking them notices the shape. He does not.

**7 — The Variable Accounted For** (play, ~2400)
A plan fails at the exact wrong moment. Combat is defensive — the first fight in the game he did not
author. Human proxies only. Nothing divine on screen, per the pitch.

**8 — Something Is Playing** (interlude)
He concludes there is an opponent. Not a god — an opponent. Restraint rule: he must not be frightened
and must not be exhilarated. He is *interested*, and that is worse.

### Act III — The Law Revised

**9 — It Should Have Worked** (play, ~2600)
**Revision QTE debut.** Mid-input, the ability being used stops existing — the prompt changes under
the player's thumb rather than demanding a faster reaction. Uses the existing HP-threshold QTE
architecture (`js/smb-qte-core.js` pivot design) as reference, not as a dependency.

**10 — A Copy Of What It Was** (ledger)
First ledger chapter. Not a place yet — a record. UI is a page, not a room. He banks the first
deprecated law. Teaching chapter for the mechanic, no combat.

**11 — Planetary** (read+play, ~3600)
Region scale; the act where canon's "planetary threat" becomes literally true. First in-combat ledger
re-invocation: hold an old law live for a few seconds and win inside that window.

**12 — The Entry That Isn't There** (interlude)
He reaches for a banked law and the entry is missing. He re-files it and moves on in the same breath.
No music change, no camera move, no line about it.

### Act IV — The Room Outside

**13 — The Room Outside** (ledger/explore, ~5000)
The pocket dimension as an explorable space. Authored and cluttered by hand — shelves of laws that no
longer apply anywhere, each one a thing he lost on the surface. Structural reference is the main
game's domain spaces, but the feel must be *personal*, not systemic.

**14 — Arguments About Physics** (play, ~2800)
Combat against constructs made of contested law. Each opponent asserts a rule; the ledger asserts a
different one; the arena runs on whoever is currently winning the argument.

**15 — Declining To Cooperate** (interlude)
God's method at full expression and still invisible: the world simply stops working for him in one
more specific way. Show it through a small domestic failure, not a catastrophe.

### Act V — Quarantine

**16 — Quarantine** (play, ~3000)
The terms close. The level should read as a shrinking of options rather than a wall — verbs going
away, not geometry. He moves himself outside the walls; the cost is his body coming unmoored from a
world it no longer belongs to. Play it as loss, never as a power-up.

**17 — One Skull Was Not Enough** (interlude)
Construction of the second brain. The single warmest sequence in the game, deliberately. He is tired.
He is alone. He is building the thing that lets him stop carrying all of it by himself. The audience
must finish this chapter thinking *of course he did*.

**18 — The Last Proxy** (read+play, ~3000)
Final surface fight. He wins it the way he wins everything — the read phase decides it — and the
combat is almost a formality. That flatness is intentional and is the last thing the game says about
him before the ending.

**19 — He Read It As Victory** (interlude)
God withdraws. No final blow. He asks the second brain a question he has asked ten thousand times.
The answer is a half-second late. He does not remark on it; neither does the game. Then the credits,
on his read, uncorrected.

**Hard rule, from canon and the pitch:** do not show the Void Mind leaving, do not correct his read,
do not name the homeworld, do not resolve whether it survived.

## ART DIRECTION — WHY THE PREQUELS DON'T LOOK LIKE THE MAIN GAME

Decided September 2026, after the Sovereign skeleton inherited the Axiom Prequel's look by accident
(same fork). Keeping the divergence, but making it deliberate, because it is doing real work:

**The main game is present tense.** Its direction is grounded and premium — value ramps, desaturated
materials, one accent at inlay scale. It should feel like it is happening to the player right now.

**Both prequels are records of things that already happened.** Flat masses, near-monochrome plus a
single light source, typewriter type, no HUD chrome that a live game would have. That reads as
*recovered*, not *live* — which is the correct register for a game whose entire ending depends on the
audience knowing more than the protagonist does.

**But Sovereign must not look like Axiom either.** Same grammar, different record:

| | Axiom Prequel | Sovereign Prequel |
|---|---|---|
| When | decades before the main story | ~50,000 years before |
| Place | human-scale, urban, interior-adjacent | pre-industrial, open, horizon-dominated |
| Light | cold, grey, street-lit, hard-edged | warm, dusty, firelight and sun, no hard edges |
| Ground | pavement, right angles | earth, water, slope |

So: shared grammar says "this is a record"; palette and silhouette vocabulary say *which* record.
Practically, in `js/rendering.js`, this lives in `BG_PALETTES` — every palette declares `far` as the
lightest mass (atmospheric perspective), `hill` mid, figures darkest, and a `haze` band of lit air at
foot height. Without that haze band everything at the actors' height collapses into one value and the
figures disappear; that is the single most important rule in this game's rendering.

## MECHANICS — WHAT HAS TO BE BUILT

Three systems, each mapping to a chapter `type`. All three sit on top of the existing fighter/arena
pipeline; none of them replaces it.

### 1. THE READ (`type: 'read'` prefix on a play chapter)
A pre-fight phase. The camera detaches, combat input is disabled, and the player watches the encounter
area run on a loop: patrol paths, a rival's temper meter, a market's flow. The player commits exactly
**one** structural edit from a small chapter-authored list (open a gate, move a crate, pay a man, be
somewhere visible). The fight then starts with that edit applied.

- Schema addition: `read: { loopFrames, edits: [{ id, label, apply }] }` on the chapter.
- The edit is a mutation of the chapter's own spawn/platform data, applied before the fight builds.
- Skill expression is the setup. The fight itself should be winnable but not free without the edit.
- Only one edit, always. The moment it is two, this becomes a build menu and stops being a read.

### 2. THE LEDGER (`type: 'ledger'`, plus a persistent global)
A bank of deprecated laws. Each entry is a rule God has revised out, held as a working copy.

- Persistent across chapters; the game's only progression system.
- In combat: hold a key to re-assert one banked law locally for ~3 seconds (suggested: gravity as it
  used to be, a weapon that no longer exists, a body that used to be able to take that hit).
- Costs: one use per entry per fight, and a visible wind-down. It should feel like *cheating with
  something borrowed*, not like a super meter.
- Entries are acquired in `ledger` chapters and lost silently — see chapter 12.

### 3. THE REVISION QTE
The endgame's confrontation grammar, and the reason God never gets a health bar.

- A QTE where the **prompt itself is edited mid-input**: the button you were told to press stops being
  a thing you can do. Failure is not slow reflexes; failure is trusting the terms.
- Reference the existing pivot-point QTE design (`js/smb-qte-core.js`) for structure only — this game
  is a separate codebase, same as `axiom-prequel/`.
- **No god-bar, ever.** God is never an entity with health in this game.

## BUILD PLAN

Sibling game, own folder, same relationship to the main repo as `axiom-prequel/` — separate codebase,
plain globals, ordered `<script>` tags, no build step, design reference only in both directions.

```
sovereign-prequel/
  index.html
  style.css
  js/ globals.js  input.js  camera.js  particles.js  rendering.js
      combat.js   enemies.js  sovereign.js  proxies.js
      read.js      ← the read phase
      ledger.js    ← the ledger bank + in-combat re-invocation
      qte.js       ← the revision QTE
      story.js     ← CHAPTERS[] (this document)
      cinematics.js  interlude.js  menu.js  loop.js
```

Order of work — each step playable before the next starts:

1. ~~Fork the Axiom Prequel's skeleton and get one empty walk-forward chapter running.~~ **DONE**
2. ~~Chapters 0, 2, 4 — interludes only.~~ **DONE** — the three Act I interludes play, and
   `_completeChapter` skips unbuilt chapters rather than renumbering them.
3. ~~`read.js` + chapter 1.~~ **DONE — gate passed.** The read runs observe → choose → commit → fight,
   the four edits are genuine tradeoffs (one delays two men, one buys a chokepoint, one removes a man
   but angers the rest, one stages their arrival at the cost of your back), and the fight resolves
   from whatever the edit left behind. Minimal combat (`js/combat.js`) exists to make a read's
   consequences legible, nothing more.
4. ~~Chapters 3, 5, 7 (+6) — Act I/II complete on read+combat alone.~~ **DONE.** Chapters 0–7 all
   play; ids 8–19 remain declared-but-unbuilt. Act II's shape is now load-bearing: ch5 is the same
   one-edit read at town scale (the escalation is in the SIZE OF THE CONSEQUENCE, never in the number
   of edits), ch6 plants three unremarked failures, and **ch7 deliberately has no read at all** — the
   missing phase, the empty edit slot in the fight HUD, and the enemies arriving from behind are the
   chapter's entire statement.
5. ~~`qte.js` + chapter 9.~~ **DONE.** Chapter 9 runs two QTEs: the first is CLEAN and teaches the
   grammar, the second is the same sequence with the key revoked mid-hold. Failure is trusting the
   instruction, not being slow — the revoked key stays on screen struck through, and the fail text is
   *"Nothing was wrong with how you did it."*
6. ~~`ledger.js` + chapters 10, 11, 12.~~ **DONE.** `LEDGER_DEFS` holds three laws; `ledgerMod(kind,
   base)` is the single place the rest of the game asks what is currently true, and it applies **to him
   only** — the working copy is his and he is the one running it, so a re-asserted law is not a free
   buff for the room. The ledger arc across the game: ch10 files two, **ch12 silently takes one**
   (`loses:`, with no flash, sound or line — the same HUD element the player already knows simply has
   one fewer row), ch13 re-files it from memory and adds a third.
7. ~~Act IV (13–15) — the pocket space.~~ **DONE.** The pocket gets its own background treatment
   (`structures: 'room'` → stone courses, uneven lamp placement, no horizon) because drawing the
   village rooftops in there made the archive read as one more outdoor level. Ch14 makes contested law
   literal: an enemy with `asserts:` holds a rule in force while it lives, and the ledger **contests**
   it rather than ignoring it — whoever is running a copy wins locally.
8. ~~Act V (16–19).~~ **DONE.** Ch16's walls are `revokes:` — verbs going missing on a timer
   (the ledger will not open; the ground stops letting go of you), never a difficulty spike. Ch19 ends
   on his victory read, uncorrected, and carries the seam: a question he has asked ten thousand times,
   `delayBefore: 34` frames of real silence, then the answer in the inner voice. Nothing remarks on it.

**All 20 chapters play.** The game is content-complete end to end and runs 0→19 with no errors.
Still missing: audio of any kind, and a credits/ending screen past the final hold.

Step 3 was the go/no-go gate and it passed: the read carries the fight. Everything after it assumes
that. Files as built: `globals, input, camera, particles, figure, actor, combat, story, interlude,
read, ledger, qte, rendering, menu, loop` + `index.html` + `style.css`. Script tags carry a `?v=` suffix from the
start — Chrome's disk cache will otherwise serve stale JS and cost an hour chasing a ghost.
